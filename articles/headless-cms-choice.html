Choosing a Headless CMS: Start With the Business

Content modelling, integrations and ownership matter more than a fashionable platform name.

Adapted for this website from an earlier LinkedIn article. LinkedIn ↗

01

The decision starts before the software demo

Choosing a CMS can look like a technical procurement exercise. In practice, the decision shapes the work of content editors, developers and product teams for years. It is worth starting with a simple question: what does the business actually need to publish, change and manage?

A good requirements workshop often reveals more than a polished product demonstration. The publishing process, approvals, multiple languages and the need to reuse content across channels all influence the outcome.

02

What I would evaluate

I look at content models, API capabilities, integration with the existing architecture, editor experience, permissions, search visibility and the operating model after launch. The cost is not only the licence fee; it includes implementation, maintenance and the time people spend working around unnecessary complexity.

Strapi, Contentful, Sanity and other headless options can all make sense, but not for the same organisation. Their value depends on how well the product fits the team and the systems around it.

03

Headless is an architectural choice, not a shortcut

Separating content from presentation gives teams freedom to deliver different experiences. It also moves more responsibility into the frontend, infrastructure, preview workflows and integration architecture. If those responsibilities are not understood, flexibility can become overhead.

For eCommerce, I pay particular attention to the boundary between marketing content and transactional data. Product information, price, availability and checkout belong to a broader ecosystem. The CMS should work with that ecosystem rather than become a second source of truth.

04

My conclusion

The best CMS is the one the organisation can actually operate well. I would rather choose a smaller, sustainable solution that editors understand and engineers can maintain than an impressive platform that introduces a dependency every time somebody needs to publish a page.

The right technology creates room for better decisions. It should never be the strategy by itself.
